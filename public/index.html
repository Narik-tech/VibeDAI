How Classical search uses these settings
Complete turns and present boards
Search builds legal submitted turns, including several component moves when required. Ordinary moves are considered on boards required to advance the present; optional boards contribute cross-board moves.
Iterative deepening and principal variation search
Depth grows one complete turn at a time. Alpha-beta search tries the leading continuation first, then probes alternatives with narrow windows and re-searches promising alternatives.
Aspiration windows and ordering
Later iterations begin near the previous score and widen the window when needed. Captures, promotions, cached moves, killers, quiet history and spatial centralization guide the order; their bonuses are not position-evaluation points.
Quiescence and check evasions
Capture and promotion extensions reduce abrupt tactical cutoffs. At the configured checked horizon, search examines one further legal evasion and tests its resulting position for a terminal outcome.
Transposition cache
Search reuses matching positions with their complete history. Normal and tactical entries respect their search depth or horizon, and remain subject to the configured RAM budget.
Royal safety and pruning
Proven royal threats allow exact unsafe-turn pruning. Legal submission still requires every royal to be safe; heuristic exposure scores do not decide legality. Full-rule verification is required before claiming mate or stalemate.

There is no opening book, tablebase, repetition adjudication, null-move assumption or normal-search beam cap. Finite depth and the optional-board policy can still miss tactics. Default weights are hand tuned; the experimental alternatives were not retained.
